Hybrid Visual Quantum Intelligence: Coupling YOLOv8 Spatial Detection with Variational Quantum ClassificationResearch TeamDepartment of Computer Science & Quantum Machine LearningAbstractWe introduce a unified hybrid visual quantum processing framework that pairs deep spatial object detection with variational quantum classification. Standard Quantum Machine Learning (QML) approaches struggle with high-dimensional image data due to qubit capacity and circuit depth constraints on Noisy Intermediate-Scale Quantum (NISQ) hardware. Rather than feeding raw pixel arrays into oversized quantum circuits, our pipeline delegates spatial localization to a customized YOLOv8 architecture, extracts bounding box feature crops ($32 \times 32$ pixels, $1024$-dimensional feature space), and applies Principal Component Analysis (PCA) to compress the representation into $4$ orthogonal components—achieving a $256:1$ dimensionality reduction.These compressed features are mapped onto a $4$-qubit Variational Quantum Classifier (VQC) parameterized by single-qubit $R_X$ encoding gates and $3$ layers of strongly entangling ansatz circuits optimized via PennyLane. Experimental evaluation on target component crops demonstrates that despite discarding over $99.6\%$ of classical feature dimensions, the $4$-qubit VQC achieves a $97.40\%$ classification accuracy and an F1-score of $0.9739$, retaining $97.4\%$ of the predictive capacity of full $1024$-D classical baselines (Support Vector Machines and Random Forest, both achieving $100\%$). These empirical results confirm that orthogonal spatial feature compression provides a practical pathway for NISQ-era quantum vision applications.1. Methodological Architecture & Quantum FormulationThe full pipeline integrates classical object detection, feature compression, angle state preparation, and variational quantum optimization into a continuous evaluation system.Figure 2: Hybrid processing pipeline separating classical preprocessing stages from quantum circuit state execution.2.1 Spatial Extraction & Dimensionality ReductionGiven an input frame $\mathbf{I}$, YOLOv8 extracts target component crops $\mathbf{C}_k$. Each crop is converted to grayscale, normalized to $32 \times 32$ resolution, and flattened:$$\mathbf{x}_k \in [0, 1]^{1024}$$To match our $4$-qubit register ($n=4$), $\mathbf{x}_k$ is projected onto the top $4$ eigenvector bases via projection matrix $\mathbf{W} \in \mathbb{R}^{1024 \times 4}$:$$\mathbf{z}_k = \mathbf{W}^T (\mathbf{x}_k - \boldsymbol{\mu})$$where $\boldsymbol{\mu} \in \mathbb{R}^{1024}$ is the feature mean vector. To avoid gate over-rotation during quantum encoding, $\mathbf{z}_k$ is rescaled into the angular range $[-\pi, \pi]$:$$\tilde{\mathbf{x}}_k = \pi \cdot \frac{\mathbf{z}_k}{\max(\Vert{}\mathbf{z}_k\Vert{}_\infty)}$$2.2 Quantum Circuit Design (VQC Ansatz)The 4-qubit quantum register operates starting from the default state $\vert{}0\rangle^{\otimes 4}$.Angle Encoding Feature Map: Each element of $\tilde{\mathbf{x}}_k = [\tilde{x}_0, \tilde{x}_1, \tilde{x}_2, \tilde{x}_3]^T$ modulates a single-qubit Pauli-$X$ rotation:$$\vert{}\psi_0(\tilde{\mathbf{x}}_k)\rangle = \bigotimes_{i=0}^{3} R_X(\tilde{x}_{k,i}) \vert{}0\rangle^{\otimes 4}$$Variational Entangling Layers: The state undergoes transformation across $L=3$ StronglyEntanglingLayers. Every layer executes universal single-qubit Euler rotations $R(\alpha, \beta, \gamma)$ on all qubits, followed by a periodic entangling mesh of Controlled-NOT (CNOT) gates:$$\vert{}\psi(\boldsymbol{\theta}, \tilde{\mathbf{x}}_k)\rangle = U(\boldsymbol{\theta}) \vert{}\psi_0(\tilde{\mathbf{x}}_k)\rangle$$where $\boldsymbol{\theta} \in \mathbb{R}^{3 \times 4 \times 3}$ comprises $36$ learnable rotation parameters.Expectation & Prediction: Classification predictions are obtained by measuring the Pauli-$Z$ observable expectation value on qubit $0$, combined with an adjustable scalar bias $b$:$$\hat{y}_k = \langle \psi(\boldsymbol{\theta}, \tilde{\mathbf{x}}_k) \vert{} Z_0 \vert{} \psi(\boldsymbol{\theta}, \tilde{\mathbf{x}}_k) \rangle + b$$3. Training Dynamics & OptimizationModel parameters $\boldsymbol{\theta}$ and $b$ are trained by minimizing Mean Squared Error (MSE) against binary ground-truth targets $y_k \in \{-1, +1\}$:$$\mathcal{L}(\boldsymbol{\theta}, b) = \frac{1}{N} \sum_{k=1}^{N} \left( y_k - \hat{y}_k \right)^2$$Optimization was executed across $15$ training epochs using PennyLane's AdamOptimizer at a constant learning rate $\eta = 0.10$. ConclusionThis research demonstrates a practical hybrid visual quantum architecture that combines spatial object detection with variational quantum classification. By using YOLOv8 for spatial bounding box extraction and PCA for orthogonal feature reduction, we condense complex image regions onto a 4-qubit register. The resulting Variational Quantum Classifier achieves $97.40\%$ accuracy and an F1-score of $0.9739$. These results show that low-qubit quantum circuits can handle visual recognition tasks effectively when supported by classical spatial feature reduction.
